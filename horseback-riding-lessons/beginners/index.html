Beginner Horseback Riding Lessons in Harwood, MD

Your First Time, With Zero Judgment

You belong here

The three worries we hear every week

If one of these is quietly holding you back from a first horseback riding lesson, read on — because none of it is the obstacle you think it is.

The worry

“I'm too old, or not fit enough.”

The truth

We teach riders from childhood into their seventies, and riding itself builds the balance and core strength you need as you go.

The worry

“I have literally never touched a horse.”

The truth

Perfect — that is exactly who beginner lessons are for. Your very first lesson starts on the ground, not in the saddle.

The worry

“I'll be nervous and embarrass myself.”

The truth

There is no crowd and no judgment here — just you, a calm horse and a patient instructor. Nervous first-timers are our favorite riders to teach.

Why beginners thrive here

Built for the very first lesson

We start from truly zero

No experience needed and none assumed. We begin on the ground, meeting and grooming your horse, long before you ever pick up a trot.

Calm horses, calm pace

Our beginner horses are chosen for patience. Nothing happens before you are ready — there is no rushing and no pressure to keep up.

A teacher in your corner

Savanna has guided hundreds of first-timers. Lessons are private or semi-private, so every single minute is about you.

What actually happens

Your first hour, minute by minute

Wondering what you have signed up for? Here is how a typical first horseback riding lesson unfolds — calm, structured and never rushed.

  1. First 10–20 min

    Meet & groom your horse

    You will learn to safely approach, groom and tack up — the quiet rituals that build trust before you ever climb aboard.

  2. Next

    Mount & find your seat

    Using a mounting block you will get on, settle into a tall, balanced posture, and learn how to hold the reins.

  3. Then

    Walk, steer & halt

    In our enclosed sand ring you will ask your horse to walk on, steer and halt. Most first-timers are walking confidently by the end of the hour.

  4. To finish

    End on a high note

    We always finish on a good moment, never a scary one — and most beginners are already planning their next visit.

6–10 lessons

to ride confidently at the walk

Most beginners are comfortable steering and controlling a horse at the walk within roughly six to ten weekly lessons, and many are trotting within a couple of months. Everyone is different — and we never push your pace.

Source: The Riding Instructor — how long it takes to learn to ride
The honest logistics

What to wear, and what we provide

Wear long pants — leggings or jeans are ideal — and closed-toe shoes or boots with a small heel. That is genuinely all you need to begin.

We provide a clean, certified helmet for your first few lessons, then help you choose your own for the best fit.

Plan to be at the barn about 30 minutes beyond your saddle time, for grooming and tacking up — a hands-on part of the bond, not a chore.
