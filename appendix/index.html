Sources & further reading

Where our guidance comes from

The educational articles on our blog draw on widely accepted, mainstream horsemanship knowledge and the lived experience of our instructor, who brings more than 20 years of riding and over 15 years of teaching. For readers who wish to explore further, we recommend consulting reputable, authoritative sources directly, such as:

  • Equine veterinary professionals — for any guidance specific to horse health, feeding, hydration and seasonal care. A qualified equine veterinarian should always guide decisions about an individual horse.
  • Helmet safety standards bodies — for current equestrian helmet certification standards, such as ASTM International and the Safety Equipment Institute (SEI).
  • Anne Arundel County Recreation & Parks — for current rules, maps and access information for designated horse trails in county parks.
  • The Maryland Horse Industry Board and the Maryland Department of Agriculture — for state-level information on the horse industry, licensed stables and equine resources.
  • Recognized equestrian and youth-riding organizations — for general guidance on lessons, rider development and safe practice.

Where our articles describe age guidelines, gear recommendations or care practices, these reflect commonly accepted advice rather than rigid rules. Every rider, child and horse is an individual, and we encourage families to speak with us or with relevant professionals about their specific situation.

Accuracy & content notes
