How to Choose a Horseback Riding Helmet for Kids & Adults

The One Piece of Gear That Truly Matters

Gear & What to Wear November 23, 2025 7 min read By Savanna

Choosing a horseback riding helmet for kids and adults is the single most important gear decision any rider makes.

A helmet is not optional equipment — it is the foundation of riding safely.

Look for a certified riding helmet

A horseback riding helmet is specifically designed for the kind of falls riders experience. Look for a helmet certified to an equestrian safety standard, such as ASTM/SEI certification.

A bicycle helmet is not a substitute, because it is built for different impacts.

Fit is everything

A helmet only protects if it fits. It should sit level and low on the forehead, feel snug all the way around without pinching, and stay put when you shake your head before the chin strap is even fastened.

The strap should be comfortably firm under the chin.

Helmets for growing children

Children's heads grow, so check the fit regularly. Many youth helmets include an adjustment dial that extends their useful life.

Never buy a helmet a child must "grow into" — a loose helmet does not protect.

When to replace a helmet

Replace any helmet after a significant fall or impact, even if it looks fine, because the protective material may be compressed. Most manufacturers also suggest replacement every few years with regular use.

What kind of helmet do you need for horseback riding?

You need a helmet specifically certified for equestrian use, such as one carrying ASTM/SEI certification. A bicycle or skate helmet is not a safe substitute. The helmet must fit snugly and level on the head, and it should be replaced after any significant fall.

Key takeaways

  • Use a helmet certified for equestrian use, not a bike helmet.
  • Fit must be snug, level and secure — never loose.
  • Check children's helmet fit often as they grow.
  • Replace any helmet after a significant impact.
